# GoodBuy Foods Global: business plan review (November 2026)

## 1. What was wrong with the earlier plan

| Issue | Why it hurts | Correction made |
|---|---|---|
| **Paid by both sides.** The site said "we work for the buyer" while the supplier page said GFG earns commission from suppliers. | A buyer who finds out stops trusting every rating and QC release. | Supplier commission removed everywhere. Buyers pay one published fee. |
| **Too broad to be found or trusted.** 140 products presented equally, with no clear customer. | A new broker cannot be credible in everything, and search engines rank specific pages, not general ones. | Three focus segments named. Every product and market now has its own page. |
| **Described services, not problems.** | Buyers search for their problem ("shrimp rejected at EU border"), not for a broker. | Site rebuilt around ten buyer problems and eight supplier problems. |
| **Nothing for sellers.** | Good suppliers have no reason to prioritise GFG's buyers. | Supplier benefits spelled out: verified buyers, QC evidence that supports price, no commission, forecasts. |
| **No answer to changing rules.** | Traceability and trade-preference changes are the next reasons buyers will switch suppliers. | Digital lot file and a regulatory watch added. |

## 2. The corrected model

**Position:** the buyer's sourcing office in Bangladesh. Paid only by buyers. Independent of suppliers.

**Revenue, in order of importance**

1. **Supply programmes (recurring).** Monthly or quarterly containers for the same buyer. This is the profit engine: the supplier is verified once, QC becomes routine, and revenue repeats without new sales cost.
2. **Complete (1.5% of FOB, minimum US$450 per container).** The main per-shipment product.
3. **Assure (US$290 per container) and Verify (US$150 per supplier).** Low-priced entry products. Their job is to win the first order and convert the buyer to Complete or a programme.
4. **Export-Ready Pack for suppliers (US$200, one-time, proposed).** Spec sheets, photos and a document checklist. It never affects a rating.

**Rough unit economics (assumptions to replace with real figures)**

| Shipment | FOB value | Fee at 1.5% | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40′ reefer of black tiger shrimp | about US$170,000 | about US$2,550 | Healthy. One QC officer-day plus travel is covered several times over. |
| 40′ reefer of frozen fish | about US$60,000 | about US$900 | Acceptable. |
| Container of potato | about US$8,000 | US$450 (minimum) | Thin. Only worth doing inside a programme. |
| Assure only, plant in Khulna | n/a | US$290 | Likely below cost if a trip is made for one container. Combine visits or use a local inspector. |

**Decisions for the Chairman**

- **Fees.** They are set low, as instructed. Assure at US$290 will lose money on single visits outside Dhaka. Options: keep it as a loss-leading entry offer, raise it to about US$390, or charge travel at cost.
- **Supplier commission.** Removed, because it conflicts with the buyer-side promise. Taking it back would raise short-term income and cost long-term trust.
- **Refund guarantee.** It is limited to GFG's own fee, which keeps the exposure small. Do not extend it to the value of the goods.

## 3. Where to focus first

| Segment | Why | Lead products |
|---|---|---|
| Seafood importers in the EU, UK and Gulf | Highest value per container; compliance is their biggest fear | Black tiger shrimp, golda prawn, pomfret, ribbonfish |
| South Asian grocery wholesalers (UK, Gulf, North America, Malaysia, Italy) | Under-served: they need many small lines in one container, which exporters avoid | Freshwater fish, dried fish, aromatic rice, puffed rice, spices, frozen paratha |
| Food service and private label | Sticky, specification-driven, recurring | Pangasius fillet, value-added shrimp, frozen vegetables, mango pulp |

Hilsa and aromatic rice draw attention but are export-restricted. Use them to attract enquiries, not as core volume. Live crab and eel for China need GACC-registered exporters; treat as a second phase.

## 4. Growth without paid ads

- **158 pages** now target specific searches: one per product ("black tiger shrimp from Bangladesh"), one per market ("import from Bangladesh to the United Kingdom"), plus the guide and solutions pages.
- Each page answers the question in its first lines and carries structured data, which is what search engines and AI assistants quote.
- All pages come from one catalogue file, so adding a product or market adds a page.
- Next steps outside the site: Google Search Console and Bing submission, Google Business Profile, listings on BFFEA/EPB directories, LinkedIn posts by the Chairman, and one buyer case study as soon as the first shipment completes.

## 5. Scaling and efficiency

- **Automate the routine.** The n8n workflows already capture, score and route every buyer and supplier enquiry. Next: automatic quote reminders and the digital lot file checklist.
- **Inspector network instead of headcount.** Use vetted freelance QC inspectors in Khulna, Chattogram and Cox's Bazar, paid per visit, under one GFG checklist.
- **Standardise.** One specification template, one inspection report and one lot file per shipment.
- **Proof before breadth.** Complete three shipments in one segment, publish the evidence, then widen.

## 6. Risks to manage

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Legal status | Confirm the right registration for a buying agent in Bangladesh (indenting or commission-agent registration, trade licence) with a lawyer before invoicing. |
| Liability for goods | Contracts must state that the exporter is the seller and GFG is the buyer's agent. |
| Buyers and suppliers bypassing GFG | NCNDA, plus real value on every shipment (QC, documents, claims). |
| Trade preferences | Bangladesh's LDC graduation is scheduled for 24 November 2026 and a deferral has been requested. Check duty per shipment. |
| Unproven claims | "World's best and most trusted" is stated as a mission. Replace it with evidence as shipments complete. |
