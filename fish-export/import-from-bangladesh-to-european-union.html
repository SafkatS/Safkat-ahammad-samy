Importing food and seafood from Bangladesh to the European Union

The approvals, documents and checks your shipment needs, and how we make sure they are in place before loading.

In short: Seafood must come from a plant on the EU list of approved establishments for Bangladesh. Sea freight takes 28–35 days to Rotterdam, Antwerp or Hamburg.

Approvals and rules

  • Seafood must come from a plant on the EU list of approved establishments for Bangladesh.
  • Residue monitoring must meet EU rules; maximum levels are set by Regulation (EU) 2023/915.
  • Wild-caught fish needs a catch certificate under the EU IUU regulation.
  • Fresh produce needs a phytosanitary certificate; mango comes from monitored orchards.

Duty and preferences

Bangladeshi goods currently enter under the Everything But Arms scheme. Preferences are expected to continue for three years after Bangladesh's LDC graduation, then move to another scheme. Confirm the duty for your HS code.

Rules and tariffs change. This guide is a starting point, not legal or customs advice. Updated 2026-11-12.

Documents

  • Health certificate issued by the Department of Fisheries (seafood)
  • Common Health Entry Document through TRACES at the border control post
  • Catch certificate for wild-caught marine fish
  • Phytosanitary certificate (fresh produce)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading and certificate of origin

Common reasons shipments are held or rejected

  • Antibiotic residues (nitrofurans, chloramphenicol) in shrimp
  • Plant missing from the approved list
  • Pesticide residues above MRLs on fresh produce
  • Incomplete catch certificates
